Family Health Planner — PRD
Sep 18, 2026 · @Phil Heijkoop
1. Overview
The Family Health Planner produces one accepted weekly plan per household: dinners (with lunch awareness) for four people, and Mon–Fri training plans for two adults. Plans are generated by one button press, reviewed, then accepted as-is, revised, or replaced manually. It runs as a private app on the home.madebyphil.com Tailscale platform.
Problem. Evening decision fatigue: what's for dinner, is it in the house, what's today's workout. Plans today are ad hoc and nothing learns from last week.
Goals (v1)
• One place to see this week's dinners, lunches-from-leftovers, and each adult's workouts.
• Every dinner has a recipe and every ingredient is on a grocery list tied to a specific shopping trip.
• Each new plan is informed by long-term goals, last week's accepted plan, and structured feedback on how it went.
• Meal and fitness plans are generated together and aware of each other, but can be regenerated independently (e.g. travel week).
• A growing record of meals that worked, meals that didn't, and ideas that were rejected, so suggestions improve over time.
• Enough structured tracking (weight, mood, enjoyment, portions, logged sets) to draw progress graphs.
Non-goals (v1)
• Calorie/macro tracking at the food-log level. Plans may state targets; the app does not log intake.
• Fridge inventory, store deals, and wearable imports (see §14).
• Medical guidance. The app plans meals and training; it does not advise on medication.

4. Planning loop
One Generate button runs up to three loops for the period: fitness for Phil, fitness for Liz, then meals. Fitness loops run in parallel; the meal loop runs after them and receives each adult's derived training summary. Any loop can also be run alone ("Regenerate meals only", "Regenerate my fitness only"), e.g. for a travel week.

Rules
• Only the accepted version is stored as the plan of record. Superseded drafts are not kept.
• Rejected items and their reasons are kept (§8), since that is what future generations learn from.
• Each LLM call is logged operationally (model, token counts, cost, latency, status) for 30 days; prompts and responses are kept for the same window for debugging, then deleted.
• The grocery list is never written by the LLM. It is computed by the app from the accepted recipes (§5), so every edit or revision keeps it consistent.
• After acceptance, light edits (swap two days, mark a meal skipped, substitute an exercise) update the accepted plan without re-accepting. Replacing a meal or regenerating reopens that plan as a draft.
• A loop failure (timeout, invalid JSON after one repair retry) leaves other loops' drafts intact and shows a retry button for the failed loop.
5. Meal planning
The meal plan's job is that every evening's dinner is known in advance and its ingredients are already in the house. Breakfasts are out of scope; lunches are planned only where they come from leftovers or deliberate prep.
Plan contents
• One entry per day in the period, following that day's dinner mode (§3). Cook days get a recipe; Leftovers days point to the cooked meal they come from; Takeout / Eating out / Away days get no recipe.
• A Cook meal can be flagged cook-double, which generates the Leftovers dinner or lunches it feeds. The plan shows the link both ways ("Tue: makes Wed lunch x2").
• Lunch entries only on days with lunch mode Leftovers or Prep.
• Perishable-heavy meals are scheduled closest after a shopping trip; sturdier meals later in the gap.
• A dinner accepted in the last 3 weeks is not repeated unless marked Staple in the library (setting: repeat window, default 3).
• Ingredients should lean toward what is in season in Massachusetts for the period's month(s), using the seasonal produce table in settings (seeded, editable).

• A household staples list (settings) marks items as "check pantry" instead of "buy".
• Grouped by grocery category; tickable on a phone in the store; ticks shared between both users in real time or on refresh.
• Manual lines can be added to any trip.
6. Fitness planning
Each adult gets a private Mon–Fri plan covering only the days they marked available. Weekends are never planned; weekend activity (hikes etc.) can be logged as an activity so the next plan accounts for it.
Inputs per adult (private)
• Long-term goal record: goal statement, optional measurable target and date, constraints (injuries, niggles, movements to avoid).
• Equipment profiles: Home and Travel (seeded as bodyweight + bands + running), editable. The travel flag (§3) switches profiles.
• Session length preference (e.g. 30 / 45 / 60 min) and preferred session types (strength, run, mobility, class).
• Last period's accepted plan, logs if any, and feedback (§7).

Progression rules (in the prompt, and checked by the app where possible)
• With logs: progress from last logged performance (e.g. add weight when all prescribed reps were hit at or below the target RPE).
• Without logs (logging is optional): progress from session feedback only (completed / partly / skipped; too easy / about right / too hard), conservatively.
• A missed or "too hard" week repeats or reduces load rather than advancing.
• Plans state the week's intent in one line ("Build", "Repeat", "Deload") so the pattern is visible over time.

8. Meal library and preference memory
Every accepted recipe is saved to a shared library, and the library's verdicts are what future generations lean on or avoid.
Library entry
• The recipe (latest accepted version) and its tags.
• Stats derived from feedback: times served, last served, average enjoyment per person, oldest's acceptance rate, portion signals.
• Verdict: Staple / Worked / Needs tweak / Didn't work. Derived from feedback (e.g. average enjoyment ≥ 4 and repeat = yes → Worked), always overridable by hand.
• Tweak notes carried forward ("less spice", "serve rice separately") and applied the next time it's planned.
Rejection list
• Every rejected suggestion, with who rejected it, when, and the optional reason ("doesn't sound appealing", "too fiddly on a weeknight").
• Sent to the meal loop as "do not suggest" for 12 weeks (setting), with reasons included so the model can generalise (e.g. avoid fiddly weeknight dishes, not just that one dish).
• Viewable and clearable.
Using the library
• Browse and filter by verdict, tag, protein, kid-friendliness, season.
• "Plan this" drops a library recipe into a draft or manual plan.
• The meal loop receives a compact digest, not the whole library: Staples, the top 15 Worked meals, recent Didn't work meals with reasons, and the active rejection list.
Suggested profile updates. After Friday feedback, the meal loop may propose profile changes it has evidence for (e.g. "Oldest refused mixed sauces 3 times — add 'sauce on the side'?"). These are proposals only; a user accepts or dismisses each.

11. LLM contracts
The app assembles context, calls OpenRouter, and validates a strict JSON response against Pydantic models; the model never writes directly to the database. Use OpenRouter structured outputs (response_format: json_schema) when the chosen model supports it; otherwise the schema goes in the prompt. Invalid output gets one repair retry (the validation errors sent back), then the loop fails (§4).

Revise tasks (meals.revise, fitness.revise) receive the current draft JSON, a target (a path such as days[3].dinner or an exercise key, or plan), the feedback text and chips, and the same context. They return { "note": "...", "replacements": [{ "target": "...", "value": {...} }] } for item scope, or a full plan for plan scope. App internals
• LLM calls run as background jobs, since a generation can take 30–120 s. Use a Postgres-backed job table and an in-process worker; no Redis or extra containers. The UI shows per-loop progress and polls job status.
• One LLM client module wraps OpenRouter: config lookup, structured output, validation, repair retry, fallback model, spend cap, llm_call logging.
• Grocery aggregation, leftover linking and validation are plain, unit-tested Python. They are the parts that must be right every time.
• Mobile-first UI: the grocery list (in store), today's workout (in the gym) and meal feedback (at the table) must work one-handed on a phone.
• Seed script creates members, default settings, seasonal produce table, and v1 prompts for every task.
